Open a method to check its fit before using it. The full guide carries its practical steps, alternatives and guardrails.

L01 · Outcome delegation agreement

Transfer a bounded responsibility

Before you start: A willing recipient, authorised decision owner and a specific piece of work.

Output: A written agreement: result, decision rights, support, checkpoint and recovery owner.

Evidence: Autonomy-support and work-redesign research informs conditions; no direct test of this agreement. Decision rights, useful support and actual work conditions must change; fewer questions alone is not improvement.

Read the method
L02 · GROW coaching

Help someone choose their own next step

Before you start: Consent to a coaching conversation and a goal within their influence.

Output: A person-owned action and a review agreement, or an explicit decision not to act.

Evidence: A randomized professional-coaching bundle included GROW; broader coaching studies often exclude managers/peers. Neither the acronym alone nor this short manager-led rendition was isolated; chosen goals and evaluative power matter.

Read the method
L03 · Specific feedback with intent inquiry

Discuss observed behaviour and its effect

Before you start: First-hand observations or clearly labelled reports, time to hear the other account and an appropriate relationship.

Output: A checked or explicitly disputed account and a clear disposition.

Evidence: Feedback meta-analysis includes benefits and negative effects; perceived listening supplies adjacent context. The evidence does not validate SBI/SBII wording or predict benefit from delivering a message.

Read the method
L04 · Interest-based negotiation

Resolve incompatible demands

Before you start: Named decision owners, negotiable boundaries, relevant affected-party input and a realistic alternative if no agreement is reached.

Output: An explicit disposition with rationale and responsibilities. A reasoned no-agreement outcome can be useful.

Evidence: Conflict synthesis, conceptual negotiation readings and a publisher book excerpt clarify tradeoffs. Task conflict is not beneficial on average; these are not tests of this negotiation sequence.

Read the method
L05 · 1-2-4-All

Hear ideas beyond dominant voices

Before you start: A clear invitation, accessible participation and an explicit explanation of how input will be used.

Output: A set of ideas and unresolved concerns with a visible disposition route.

Evidence: Safety research explains risks around voice; originator guidance describes the sequence. No direct comparative effectiveness evidence for 1-2-4-All was established; participation is not safety.

Read the method
L06 · Brief and huddle

Establish or reset a shared plan

Before you start: A current goal, people involved in the work and someone able to resolve or escalate changes.

Output: A current plan with roles, unresolved constraints and checked understanding.

Evidence: Team literature informs plan coherence; handoff/charter studies are adjacent evidence. A five-minute workplace huddle is not the clinical handoff bundle or a charter intervention.

Read the method
L07 · Structured debrief

Learn from a completed episode

Before you start: A bounded episode, enough psychological safety for differing accounts and someone to own a feasible follow-up.

Output: One learning change, its rationale, owner and next check.

Evidence: Debrief meta-analysis supports an intervention family; only its abstract was inspected here. No promised local percentage improvement or detailed unread subgroup claim; hindsight can distort explanations.

Read the method
L08 · PDSA bounded test

Test an uncertain process change

Before you start: A prediction, a feasible observation and an authorised stop/recovery route.

Output: A prediction, bounded test record and a next decision.

Evidence: PDSA review appraises application/reporting; research-methods texts explain inference. It does not establish that more faithful PDSA causes better outcomes; local before/after change is confounded.

Read the method
L09 · Capacity tradeoff

Resolve work that exceeds available capacity

Before you start: Visible work/constraints and access to the person who can reprioritise, resource or stop commitments.

Output: An authorised choice about what stops, moves, changes scope or gains resources.

Evidence: Work-redesign review includes mixed and negative outcomes; HSE supplies work-design guidance. Neither a brief tradeoff discussion nor reduced workload at one point establishes system improvement.

Read the method
L10 · Evidence and consultation

Obtain cooperation across boundaries

Before you start: A clear ask, relevant evidence and access to affected voices or an honest account of missing consultation.

Output: A decision request or decision record with evidence, concerns and follow-up.

Evidence: Observational influence-tactic study provides limited, abstract-inspected support for consultation. Associations are not causal tactics or a universal ranking; outcome varies with authority relationship.

Read the method
L11 · Perspective and evidence check

Check a framing before acting

Before you start: A specific disagreement, distinguishable evidence and access to accounts or explicit acknowledgement they are missing.

Output: A corrected framing, explicit disputed facts or a targeted next inquiry.

Evidence: Perspective-getting experiments were checked at abstract level; direct accounts can supply new information. No workplace-script validation or presumption that any account is complete or true.

Read the method
L12 · Teach–Practice–Check-back

Develop a missing task skill

Before you start: A competent instructor, authorised practice conditions, adequate time and a visible performance criterion.

Output: An observed attempt, known gaps and a support/check-back plan.

Evidence: Training/transfer meta-analyses and learning chapters distinguish acquisition, use and maintained performance. Program-level moderator associations do not validate this short sequence or a universal practice schedule.

Read the method
L13 · Premortem

Surface failure risks before commitment

Before you start: A candidate plan, participants with relevant knowledge and an owner for responses.

Output: A short set of failure mechanisms with owners and responses.

Evidence: Original prospective-hindsight scenario studies concern explanation generation. They do not establish superior forecasts or success of a full team premortem.

Read the method
L14 · Closed-loop handoff

Transfer live work with checked receipt

Before you start: An identified sender and recipient, current status and a clear acceptance/escalation boundary.

Output: A received handoff with current owner, next action, risks and confirmation.

Evidence: I-PASS before/after study supports a clinical implementation bundle; team chapters add context. Check-back alone was not isolated, and pediatric error reductions cannot be promised in office work.

Read the method
L15 · Team charter and working agreements

Agree ongoing team purpose and operating norms

Before you start: A team with a legitimate remit; members can change at least some working arrangements.

Output: A short charter with working norms and a revision date. This establishes the ongoing team's operating conditions; it does not replace the existing single-decision ownership or delegation methods.

Evidence: Charter/strategy study and newer scholarly synthesis distinguish team norms from task strategy. Original study is abstract-only here and has a figure-label correction; simulations and limited evidence constrain transfer.

Read the method
L16 · Performance and learning goal design

Set a result or learning goal with feedback

Before you start: An owner who can discuss the goal, relevant constraints, feedback and realistic support.

Output: A goal, success evidence, feedback schedule, and learning requirements. The method designs the target and conditions for pursuit; GROW remains a conversation structure that can help explore them.

Evidence: Goal-setting research explains mechanisms/moderators; implementation plans address a different gap. Neither SMART phrasing nor uniformly harder targets is independently validated for this case.

Read the method
L17 · Stakeholder map and listening round

Find affected perspectives and plan meaningful involvement

Before you start: A bounded proposed change and permission for the planned listening; no implied mandate to contact anyone.

Output: A stakeholder and engagement plan grounded in actual conversations. Unlike decision ownership, this finds whose experience and participation the change requires before defining consultation rights.

Evidence: Perspective-getting and team knowledge synthesis explain why actual affected accounts matter. No comparative validation of a stakeholder grid or power/interest classification was established.

Read the method
L18 · Nominal group prioritisation

Produce a traceable group preference ranking

Before you start: A clear question, accessible private ranking, common criteria and a legitimate decision owner.

Output: A traceable prioritized list plus the criteria and dissent. Unlike 1-2-4-All or another participation structure, the defining product is independent ranking and aggregation, not simply broader contribution.

Evidence: CDC describes nominal-group procedure; safety findings identify contextual constraints. No direct outcome study of this rendition was established; preference rankings are not factual truth or population consensus.

Read the method
L19 · Driver diagram

Connect an aim to a testable improvement theory

Before you start: A bounded aim, people who understand the work and willingness to mark causal links as hypotheses.

Output: A one-page improvement theory connecting aim, drivers, and testable changes. This selects and explains the portfolio of changes; the existing PDSA method tests particular changes within it.

Evidence: PDSA reporting and measurement principles support a testable improvement theory. No direct comparative efficacy evidence for the diagram was established; arrows are hypotheses.

Read the method
L20 · Fishbone causal-hypothesis map

Organise and investigate possible contributors

Before you start: A specific observable recurring effect, relevant work knowledge and a way to verify hypotheses.

Output: A structured set of causal hypotheses and an investigation plan. A debrief examines an episode; a fishbone organizes alternative explanations for an effect, including recurring patterns.

Evidence: General research-methods explanation supports discriminating rival hypotheses. No direct fishbone efficacy evidence was added; group agreement does not identify a cause.

Read the method
L21 · Current-state process map

Understand actual steps, waits and rework loops

Before you start: A bounded start/end, access to appropriate non-sensitive examples and participation from people who do the work.

Output: A validated current-state process map with one focused improvement opportunity. Unlike a handoff protocol, this examines the end-to-end system and its loops rather than specifying one transfer.

Evidence: Measurement and positive-deviance readings inform observation and context. No direct test of this mapping protocol was established; a map may omit exceptional work or shift waits.

Read the method
L22 · Force-field change diagnosis

Identify present enablers and constraints to change

Before you start: A specific proposed change and affected perspectives; no assumption that the proposal must proceed.

Output: A change-readiness diagnosis and targeted enabling actions. Unlike a premortem, it examines forces operating now; unlike interest negotiation, it covers systemic constraints as well as stakeholder concerns.

Evidence: Review of change reactions supports separating actual conditions from deficit labels. No causal validation of force-field scores or universal change stages was established.

Read the method
L23 · If–then implementation plan

Link a chosen action to a predictable cue

Before you start: A genuinely chosen goal, an observable cue, and a feasible response within the person's authority and control. The cue may be an external event; an absent cue leaves the plan not tested.

Output: A specific cue–action commitment and a review trigger. Goal setting chooses the destination; implementation intentions pre-decide a response at the moment action is needed.

Evidence: Large experimental synthesis supports contingent plans with heterogeneous, bias-sensitive effects. Effect estimates depend on correction method; workplace transfer and component moderators are uncertain.

Read the method
L24 · Assumption map

Prioritise consequential uncertainty before testing

Before you start: An initiative with explicit assumptions, evidence to inspect and authority to prepare tests without committing them.

Output: A ranked uncertainty register and a test brief. Assumption mapping chooses what must be learned; PDSA supplies an iterative testing cycle. Neither a premortem nor a fishbone performs this importance-versus-evidence prioritization.

Evidence: Research design and PDSA principles support decision-relevant tests. No direct efficacy test of an assumption-map workshop was established.

Read the method
L25 · Positive deviance inquiry

Learn from verified better outcomes under comparable constraints

Before you start: A persistent problem, credible outcome differences, comparable resources and participant-led inquiry.

Output: A locally discovered practice and an adoption experiment with outcome monitoring. This starts from verified positive exceptions; ordinary teaching starts with known content, and general appreciative inquiry need not identify measured outperformers.

Evidence: Systematic review and critical monograph examine exemplar selection, explanation and transfer. Excellence can reflect case mix or resources; discovery does not demonstrate a transferable cause.

Read the method
L26 · Problem framing

Turn a requested solution into a bounded evidence-based problem

Before you start: A non-emergency brief with enough discretion to examine the intended outcome and affected users.

Output: A bounded problem brief with affected users, evidence, success measure, constraints, and next learning question. A perspective check challenges one interpretation; problem framing establishes what work should be attempted before selecting a method or solution.

Evidence: Framing experiments demonstrate some preference reversals; textbook concepts support diagnosis. They do not validate an entire discovery workshop or justify persuasive reframing.

Read the method
L27 · Troika peer consultation

Use rotating peer perspectives for owner-chosen action

Before you start: Three willing participants, a shareable challenge, accessible participation and truthful privacy limits.

Output: Three owner-chosen next actions and new perspectives. Unlike GROW, this is a rotating peer-consultation format; unlike 1-2-4-All, it does not build one collective answer.

Evidence: Coaching synthesis explicitly excludes peers; a scholarly comparison helps distinguish helping roles. No direct Troika trial was established; peer agreement does not prove advice or substitute for expertise.

Read the method
L28 · Individual development plan

Coordinate a supported longer-term growth pathway

Before you start: The person's aspirations, plausible opportunities, manager support and time/resources that can be genuinely committed.

Output: A jointly owned development plan with objectives, experiences, support, dates, and demonstration criteria. Teaching addresses a particular skill episode; this coordinates a longer development pathway and access to opportunities.

Evidence: Training transfer, developmental-experience studies and learning chapters support conditional development. No IDP form or fixed stretch proportion is validated; observational challenge findings are not dose prescriptions.

Read the method
L29 · Options and criteria trade-off review

Compare feasible alternatives before a significant decision

Before you start: a bounded decision, legitimate owner, materially different alternatives, evidence or explicit uncertainty, and enough time for analysis. Avoid elaborate analysis for trivial choices; unresolved criteria call for framing rather than invented weights.

Output: A decision brief with screened constraints, alternatives, evidence, uncertainty, owner and rationale.

Evidence: Framing and research-design knowledge complements NASA decision guidance. No comparative validation of this scoring/review template; natural-unit criteria and explicit values remain judgments.

Read the method
L30 · Structured listening interview

Understand and correct another person's account

Before you start: uncertainty about events or concerns, a willing participant, appropriate privacy, and capacity to listen without promising a predetermined outcome. Avoid delaying immediate protection or conducting an unauthorized investigation.

Output: A correction-ready account distinguishing agreed observations, disputes, concerns and next questions.

Evidence: Perceived-listening synthesis and perspective-getting research inform receiving an account. Predominantly observational listening associations do not prove this script improves performance.

Read the method
L31 · Change impact and support planning

Turn a defined change into a feasible transition

Before you start: a sufficiently defined proposal, named owner, known decision boundaries, affected groups who can contribute safely, and resources to address concerns. Do not pretend a fixed decision is negotiable or substitute communication for changing an unworkable proposal.

Output: An owned impact/support plan and honest communication/update commitments.

Evidence: Change-reaction review and transfer explanations distinguish support, belief and ability. No universal stage model or messaging script is established; self-reported support is not feasible execution.

Read the method
L32 · Accountable repair after one's own mistake

Acknowledge an established mistake and fulfil repair commitments

Before you start: a known action/omission one can honestly own, a suitable channel, and willingness/resources to change. Do not coach someone to confess disputed allegations, negotiate away reporting rights, or bypass formal accountability.

Output: A truthful acknowledgement of established responsibility plus feasible repair and prevention commitments.

Evidence: Scenario experiments examine perceived apology effectiveness, including null contrasts. Ratings of wording do not establish restored trust, fulfillment or forgiveness.

Use and limits

Install the skill in a compatible host and invoke it explicitly. Work with a concrete situation, the people who hold the relevant decisions, and the evidence available to them. Structural checks and model trials do not establish leadership effectiveness or human outcomes.
