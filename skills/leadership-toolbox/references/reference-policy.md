# Practical guides and supporting evidence

## Practical front door

The [method catalog](catalog.md) and its 32 method cards form the project's plain-language method wiki. They are ordinary repository Markdown pages, not a separately configured GitHub Wiki service. Read the full local card for use/avoid conditions, steps, words to try and limits. Each catalog record supplies its exact public `guide_url`, linking to an existing card (its `#try-it` section where present) on the repository's main branch. Branch-local package revisions may be newer than main until merged; do not claim the public page already contains unreleased edits.

For an ordinary method explanation or situational plan, normally include at most one relevant guide link, labelled with the method name. Do not add a bibliography to a probing question. A comparison can explain both methods in the answer and link the most useful next guide; provide more links when requested. Internal evidence reading can improve judgment without turning every response into a source list.

The index starts from common jobs; stable IDs support traceability rather than requiring readers to memorize them. Method pages put practical steps before supporting provenance. These guides are original workplace explanations except the specifically licensed L05 and L27 adaptations. Keep their CC BY-SA notices, attribution and linked license. No source text, diagram or proprietary template is to be copied into a new guide merely to avoid an external link. Do not replace an originator with an unrelated Wikipedia page or disguise provenance.

## When primary sources belong in the answer

Use relevant primary sources when the user asks for research, origin, evidence or deeper reading, and whenever the answer makes an evidence-sensitive claim: numerical effects, causal or effectiveness claims, or clinical/regulatory assertions. Practical suggestions such as “ask the receiver to repeat the next action” are editorial guidance, not a claim of proven improvement. Claims such as “this reduces errors” need actual support and a scope limit; omit or narrow them if unsupported.

Read the [evidence map](evidence-map.md), [source register](sources.md) and relevant inspected-scope record first. Distinguish procedural origin, broader constructs and tested intervention bundles. Link directly to the underlying source that supports the specific claim and state the population, setting, jurisdiction or reading limit that matters. A wiki link alone cannot substantiate empirical claims. Do not imply a linked work was fully read when only its abstract or selected section was inspected.

Healthcare and health-and-safety sources remain in the research/provenance layer. They may be appropriate for a requested evidence explanation, but they are not the default practical destination for ordinary leadership advice. Clinical bundle outcomes do not establish general workplace effectiveness; UK regulator guidance does not establish requirements elsewhere. Keep those contexts visible rather than laundering the claim through this wiki.

For urgent danger, serious allegations or a formal regulated decision, use the appropriate qualified process. Do not force a tangential method or source into that response.
