# Decisions, goals, change and learning

Reviewed 2026-10-05. Original organizing principles and source commentary; not a validated taxonomy. Read the [source roles](../sources.md), [book scopes](../books.md) and [method-specific claim limits](../evidence-map.md). Source text, figures and instruments are not included.

## Learning architecture and thematic relationships

These are original editorial organizing principles, not a validated taxonomy or new branded model.

### 1. Ask what uncertainty the next tool should reduce

A statement such as “the service is too slow” leaves several different questions unresolved: what counts as slow, where waiting occurs, why it occurs, what might change it, and whether that change is preferable to alternatives. These should not be answered by the same diagram.

- L26 establishes the decision-relevant problem and boundaries.
- L21 documents the route actually followed by work.
- L20 organizes competing explanations for a specified effect.
- L19 makes the proposed path from intervention to desired result explicit.
- L24 identifies which unsupported assumption matters enough to investigate.
- L08 exposes one prediction to a bounded real-world test.
- L29 compares the resulting options and unresolved tradeoffs.

The progression is conditional. A verified capacity shortage may justify L09 immediately; it does not require a complete diagram sequence. Measurement and experimental-design principles explain why an observed sequence, a correlated difference, and an intervention effect are different kinds of knowledge. [B40](https://kpu.pressbooks.pub/psychmethods4e/chapter/experiment-basics/), [E42](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3963536/)

### 2. Separate a goal, a strategy, and an execution cue

The target describes desired performance. The strategy explains how to produce it. The cue identifies when to execute an already chosen response. A missed deadline can arise from any of these, or from unavailable capacity or authority. Therefore a more specific target and an if–then plan are not interchangeable remedies.

Use L16 when target/feedback design is the missing condition. If no workable strategy is known, make learning visible before demanding difficult output. Use L23 when the action is feasible and wanted but a recurring opportunity is missed. [E40](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.57.9.705), [E41](https://doi.org/10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563)

### 3. Treat learning as a change in the next decision

A reflective conversation becomes operational learning when it changes a prediction, a strategy, a boundary, or a future response. Conversely, an unsuccessful test can be valuable if it rules out a consequential assumption. “The number improved” and “we learned why it changed” are separate achievements.

Link a debrief's chosen adjustment to the next comparable episode, and a test's conclusion to the next plan. Do not label a workshop productive solely because many insights were recorded. [E43](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720812448394), [E42](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3963536/)

### 4. Distinguish discovering possibilities from establishing their truth

A premortem can expand the set of imaginable failure paths. It cannot assign credible likelihoods merely by producing vivid stories. A positive-deviance inquiry can uncover candidate practices. It cannot show that copying them causes success. Both need a second stage that checks the story against competing explanations and relevant observations. [E44](https://doi.org/10.1002/bdm.3960020103), [E45](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjqs-2015-004386)

### 5. Judge transition feasibility separately from expressed support

An employee can see a benefit, dislike a loss, and still participate constructively. Another can sound supportive yet lack access, time or skill. A one-dimensional “resistance” rating collapses information the leader needs. Keep views, emotional reactions, available support and actual task performance separate. [E47](https://doi.org/10.1177/0021886310396550)


## A worked distinction (fictional)

A service misses its response target after a new intake rule. First check whether the same kinds of cases and waiting intervals were measured before and after. If staff disagree about what actually happens, observe a few ordinary and exception paths with L21. If a delay is established but its cause is unclear, L20 can hold competing explanations; L24 selects the uncertainty that changes the decision. Only when a feasible change and recovery boundary exist does L08 test a prediction. L29 then compares viable alternatives. If an obvious missing permission already explains the blockage, repair it through the legitimate owner rather than run this whole sequence.

A completed experiment, an improved average, and an explanation supported against rivals are different results. An unchanged average may hide improvement for one case type and harm for another. Preserve the denominator and balancing observations; the example illustrates judgment, not a universal measurement recipe.

# Research and reading notes

## E40

### Goals work through more than specificity

Locke & Latham (2002), theory and research-program review. [Original article](https://goal-lab.psych.umn.edu/orgPsych/2020/readings/5.%20Motivation/Locke%20%26%20Latham%20%282002%29.pdf). Full text; especially pp. 705–709 and 711–714.

Goals influence attention, effort, persistence and strategy use. Effects depend on commitment, feedback and task complexity; high output demands can obstruct learning when the performer lacks a suitable strategy. This supports diagnosing the strategy gap before selecting learning versus performance goals. It does not establish that specificity alone works or that learning goals dominate every difficult task. Copyrighted APA article; public reading access, no open reuse licence verified.

## E41

### If–then planning with calibrated expectations

Sheeran, Listrom & Gollwitzer (2025 issue; online 2024). [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/10463283.2024.2334563). Full accepted manuscript inspected via the [author-posted PDF](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Paschal-Sheeran/publication/378870694_The_When_and_How_of_Planning_Meta-Analysis_of_the_Scope_and_Components_of_Implementation_Intentions_in_642_Tests/links/65f06a571f0aec67e282f88a/The-When-and-How-of-Planning-Meta-Analysis-of-the-Scope-and-Components-of-Implementation-Intentions-in-642-Tests.pdf).

Across 642 experimental tests, ordinary pooled d=.36. Bias-adjusted estimates depend on method: trim-and-fill d=.35; robust Bayesian model averaging d=.15, interval .08–.22. The expected magnitude remains uncertain. Contingent form, motivation and rehearsal were associated with larger effects. The search ended in September 2019; substantial heterogeneity, publication bias and few workplace studies constrain transfer. Moderator comparisons can be confounded and are not universal design laws. Accepted manuscript is not the copy of record; no open reuse licence verified.

## E42

### PDSA fidelity is itself a knowledge problem

Taylor et al. (2014). [Full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3963536/). Main text, framework, results and limitations inspected; supplements not read.

The review included 73 healthcare articles. Fourteen reported linked iterative cycles; only 4/47 sufficiently detailed studies stated an explicit prediction. Critically, the review did not test whether higher methodological fidelity caused better outcomes. Its value is a sharper definition of a learning cycle and a warning against treating an acronym as evidence of experimentation. Poor reporting and poor conduct cannot always be separated. CC BY-NC 3.0; do not relicense source text under MIT.

## E43

### Debrief evidence, with an access boundary

Tannenbaum & Cerasoli (2013). [Publisher abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0018720812448394). Abstract only; full methods and moderator tables were not accessible.

The abstract reports 46 samples, N=2,136 and d=.67, and emphasizes alignment among participants, learning focus and measurement. This supports structured reflection as an evidence-backed intervention family. It does not justify promising a local 25% improvement, equating satisfaction with performance, or claiming exact facilitator/structure subgroup effects from an unread table. Treat the detailed operational additions above as editorial design. Publisher copyright.

## E44

### The premise behind a premortem has narrower support

Mitchell, Russo & Pennington (1989). [Author-uploaded original](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227768493_Back_to_the_future_Temporal_perspective_in_the_explanation_of_events). Full-text OCR; experimental methods/results and decision-aiding discussion inspected. The site’s 2006 metadata date is not the article year.

Two scenario experiments disentangled certainty from past/future perspective. Certainty changed the amount and character of explanations more strongly than temporal perspective. The authors explicitly declined to conclude that the explanations or forecasts were better. This is mechanism-relevant evidence, not validation of the complete group premortem. Avoid the popular leap from more reasons to better risk prediction. Wiley copyright; no open licence verified.

## E45

### Discovery is not dissemination or causal validation

Baxter et al. (2016). [Institutional full-text copy](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/files/62251921/Publisher%20version%20%28open%20access%29.pdf). Methods, stages, quality assessment, discussion and limitations inspected.

Thirty-seven articles covered 22 positive-deviance projects. Most attention went to identifying exemplars and generating explanations; testing and dissemination were much less developed. Weak reporting, limited comparators and limited staff/patient involvement undermine confident effectiveness conclusions. The review does not establish a pooled effect or prove that success practices transfer. CC BY-NC 4.0; link and synthesize independently.

## E46

### Equivalent descriptions can change a choice

Tversky & Kahneman (1981). [Original article, university copy](https://cool.ntu.edu.tw/files/3801495/download?download_frd=1). Full-text PDF; experiment illustrations and discussion inspected.

The experiments demonstrate systematic but non-universal preference reversals when equivalent options are described differently. Reference points and the presentation of acts, contingencies and outcomes matter. The practical implication here is to inspect consequential framing, not to manipulate people into a preferred answer. The paper does not validate the toolbox's entire problem-discovery or multicriteria procedure. AAAS copyright; no open reuse licence verified.

## E47

### Change reactions have distinct components and causes

Oreg, Vakola & Armenakis (2011). [Original review](https://bschool-en.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/businesshe/files/oreg_vakola_armenakis_2011.pdf). Full PDF available; model/methods, context/process findings and implications inspected, rather than every study row.

The review integrates 79 quantitative studies of actual organizational change. It distinguishes reactions from their antecedents and longer-term consequences, and separates affective, cognitive and behavioral reactions. Most studies were cross-sectional and nearly all used single-source self-report; no pooled causal effect was estimated. A concern can reflect the change or its management, not an employee deficit. NTL Institute copyright; no open reuse licence verified.

## B40

### Research Methods in Psychology: the inference foundation

Jhangiani, Chiang, Cuttler & Leighton (2019), fourth edition. Full chapter text inspected: [20, Reliability and Validity](https://kpu.pressbooks.pub/psychmethods4e/chapter/reliability-and-validity-of-measurement/) and [23, Experiment Basics](https://kpu.pressbooks.pub/psychmethods4e/chapter/experiment-basics/). Quasi-experiment and single-subject sections were seen only in indexed excerpts; one-group designs returned 403.

This teaches why a consistent measure may still miss the intended construct, and why changing an intervention differs from comparing pre-existing groups. It supplies general reasoning that the diagram and testing cards currently assume. Read it to understand inference, not to import psychology experiments mechanically into workplaces. CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, except noted content. Link to chapters; do not place copied text in an MIT corpus.

## B41

### Principles of Management: competing assumptions, not a script

Bright & Cortes (2019), OpenStax. Inspected [2.4](https://openstax.org/books/principles-management/pages/2-4-barriers-to-effective-decision-making), [2.5](https://openstax.org/books/principles-management/pages/2-5-improving-the-quality-of-decision-making), [10.2](https://openstax.org/books/principles-management/pages/10-2-organizational-change), [10.3](https://openstax.org/books/principles-management/pages/10-3-managing-change) and [17.6](https://openstax.org/books/principles-management/pages/17-6-employees-responses-to-planning).

The value is the contrast between routine and novel decisions and between planned and emergent change assumptions. It is explanatory teaching material, not evidence that one change-stage model wins. Current pages state CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and separately prohibit LLM ingestion/training without permission; older figure credits are inconsistent with that current notice. Keep this as a linked human reading recommendation, not a raw ingestion asset.

## B42

### The Positive Deviance Approach: a substantial critical explanation

Baxter & Lawton (2022), Cambridge Element. [Full open text](https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/positive-deviance-approach/506CA2D446210E1FE76740B7F835D87C). Inspected definitions, §2.1–2.4, §3.1–3.5, conclusions and licence.

Its strongest contribution is explaining why apparent excellence needs verification and why learning the enabling context can matter as much as copying a practice. It examines comparator selection, measurement, reverse causality, dissemination and the tension between rigor and participation. Healthcare remains a context boundary; it does not settle which elements transfer universally. CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, with third-party exceptions; link and independently synthesize rather than reproduce or adapt its text/figures.
