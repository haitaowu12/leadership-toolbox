# Understanding other people and coordinating expertise

This chapter separates an actual account, a persuasive request, and a shared operating picture. The distinctions below are editorial applications of the sources, not a tested diagnostic model.

## From mechanism to next move

- **Unknown account:** L11/L30 collects what someone actually knows or wants. Ask before interpreting hesitation as disinterest. Record what was corrected; agreement is unnecessary (E60).
- **Unsettled decision:** L10 asks the legitimate owner to weigh an explicit proposal. Genuine consultation can change the proposal. Disclose fixed constraints and preserve refusal; agreeable conversation is not commitment (E61).
- **Interdependent work:** ask what everybody needs to understand and whose specialist knowledge can remain distributed. L15 clarifies enduring interfaces, L06 resets the current plan, L14 transfers live responsibility, and L07 reviews the episode. A person can know their own job yet lack the interface information needed to coordinate (B60).

**Fictional diagnostic contrast:** A colleague repeatedly asks for approval. Before calling this a confidence problem, compare three possibilities: the authority was never transferred (L01), an exception needs expertise they cannot access (L12 or an expert), or another team's timing keeps changing (L06). Inspect the last episode, change one condition, and observe the resulting work. This is an original teaching case, not a study result.

## E60

### Perspective getting and accuracy

Eyal, Steffel & Epley (2018), *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 114(4), 547–571. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspa0000115); [author record](https://www.nicholasepley.com/publications).

**Read:** abstract only; linked PDFs were unreadable in this review. Across 25 experiments, instructions to imagine another perspective did not consistently improve accuracy. A final experiment found greater accuracy from conversation. This concerns information acquisition, not the worth of empathy. Workplace transfer and truthful disclosure under power differences are untested here. Use new accounts as evidence to evaluate, not automatically as objective facts.

## E61

### Influence across authority relationships

Yukl & Tracey (1992), *Journal of Applied Psychology*, 77(4), 525–535. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.77.4.525); [Cornell record](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/entities/publication/dac8058b-4d90-464e-86ed-e6178b6533ba).

**Read:** repository abstract and rights record, not manuscript methods. A questionnaire field study related nine influence tactics to task commitment and perceived managerial effectiveness. Rational persuasion, consultation and inspirational appeals had more favorable associations; results varied with direction of influence. It is observational evidence, not a universal tactic ranking or permission to manipulate. The repository date differs from the journal year; cite the journal year.

## B60

### Team science as a deeper reading route

National Research Council (2015), *Enhancing the Effectiveness of Team Science*, edited by Cooke & Hilton, National Academies Press. [Book DOI](https://doi.org/10.17226/19007); [Chapter 3](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/19007/chapter/7).

**Read:** specified sections of Chapter 3, pp. 59–80; not the entire book. The synthesis distinguishes common task understanding from knowing who has particular expertise. It treats teamwork as multilevel and changing over time. Task structure and interdependence affect which coordination processes matter. Read it to reason about a team’s work rather than label a leader's personality. Much underlying research concerns small teams outside science; large-team and cross-sector transfer are conditional. Public readability does not permit republishing the chapter.

## B61

### Foundational negotiation book, public excerpt

Fisher, Ury & Patton, *Getting to Yes*, Penguin revised edition (2011). [Publisher record and Chapter 4 excerpt](https://penguinrandomhousehighereducation.com/book/?isbn=9781101539545). Ebook ISBN 9781101539545; paperback 9780143118756. The displayed excerpt bears a 1991 copyright notice; it is not a review of every later revision.

**Read:** partial text, on barriers to inventing options. Useful distinction: generating possibilities and deciding what to accept are different activities. Early rejection can prevent discovery of differently valued issues; some resources nevertheless remain genuinely fixed. This is practitioner reasoning, not causal evidence for L04. No book prose, exercises or cases are reproduced.
