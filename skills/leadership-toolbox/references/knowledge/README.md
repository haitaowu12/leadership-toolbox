# Read for understanding, then choose a method

These are original, source-linked explanations of mechanisms, conditions, tensions and failure modes. Read the relevant topic before treating a familiar method name as a solution. No source proves this package's matching rubric or scripts.

## Start from the uncertainty

- **Why is someone not carrying out a task?** Read [coaching, delegation and development](coaching-development.md). Distinguish task capability, practice opportunity, resources, decision rights and the person's own chosen direction.
- **Why is teamwork or a conversation failing?** Read [feedback, voice and coordination](teams-feedback.md). Distinguish actionable task information, interpersonal risk, disagreements about interests, and interdependent work.
- **What should we decide, test or change?** Read [decisions, goals and learning](decisions-learning.md). Separate a theory of the problem, a preferred option, a test of a prediction, and learning from an episode.
- **Whose account or expertise is missing?** Read [inquiry and coordination](inquiry-coordination.md). Actual perspective, legitimate influence and shared planning are different jobs.

## Turn reading into a decision

Use a small contrast, not a theory label. Write the observed episode; name two plausible explanations only when the evidence leaves them open; identify the observation that separates them; choose the least burdensome safe way to obtain it. Then change the next action when the observation arrives. A blockage, skill deficit or unwillingness is a hypothesis until supported. A plausible mechanism is not permission to act.

Example: two late deliveries could reflect an unresolved dependency, unclear acceptance criteria or a capability gap. Ask the owner to walk through the last episode. If waiting time dominated, inspect the interface and its owner; if a demonstrated task error dominated, consider supported practice; if neither appears, keep looking or make no change. Do not promise to remove one blockage before discovering whether any exists. This is original decision guidance, not a validated diagnostic test.

Keep prerequisite status explicit where it changes advice: established, unknown or not met. Harmless preparation can proceed while execution waits for authority, consent, resources or a qualified process. A short answer can name only the decisive condition; a detailed answer can show the comparison.

## Use the library without overstating it

1. Read the topic explanation for a plausible mechanism and a rival explanation.
2. Open the relevant full method card; check safety, authority, ability and resources.
3. Use the [method evidence map](../evidence-map.md) to find what was studied, what is indirect, and what remains a gap.
4. For a consequential empirical claim, inspect the source record's access/scope and study limitations. An abstract-only record supports less detailed analysis than an inspected methods/results section. A textbook explanation is not an intervention trial.
5. Explain the actual workplace transfer as an editorial choice, and observe both intended change and burden locally.

[Sources](../sources.md) is the library entry point; [books](../books.md) identifies editions and exactly what was read. Stable E identifiers name research records, B identifiers name books/chapters, and P identifiers retain descriptive method provenance. They are identifiers, not a ranking.

A well-written provider page may be the right source for a procedure's origin. A research synthesis can explain its wider mechanism without proving that exact procedure. Neither source prestige, accessibility nor number of citations substitutes for fit to the current problem.
