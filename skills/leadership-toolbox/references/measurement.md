# Feedback that helps the next decision

Choose observations with the people affected. Use plain definitions and modest collection effort. Keep these after-action observations separate from the editorial context comparison in [matching](matching.md). Never convert them into a person score, success probability or universal ranking.

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Immediate output | What the action produced: accepted handoff, explicit tradeoff, observed practice, decision or unresolved concern |
| Later outcome signal | One desired change; definition, baseline or “unknown,” observation window, source/observer and review date |
| Balancing guardrail | One cost or harm that could rise: hidden overtime, support load, quality loss, excluded input or displaced work; locally agreed stop threshold |
| Review decision | Retain, adapt, switch, stop, no intervention or gather evidence, with the reason and owner |

Before action, write a prediction as a claim to test, not a percentage: “After the plan reset, each affected person can explain their next action at Friday’s checkpoint.” If the baseline is unavailable, say so. A completed conversation is an output; later task change is a different observation. An observed before/after difference can have other causes.

For counts, define the denominator and period. For timing, define start/end events. For ratings, state the question and whose subjective account it is. Disagreement is evidence to retain, not a score to average away. Avoid surveillance, collecting sensitive detail or mandatory surveys for trivial actions. A small qualitative check is often enough.

**Example (fictional):** A trial reduces meeting length from a locally observed 40 minutes to 25 across two meetings, but support preparation rises from 10 to 35 minutes. The output is a shorter meeting; the burden guardrail suggests adapting or stopping. Two observations do not establish causality or justify automatic scale-up.

Card signals are prompts to define locally. Their wording and thresholds are editorial, not validated instruments. Read [sources](sources.md) for what the source actually supports. [Practice template](../templates/practice-record.md) keeps prediction, observation and decision separate.

## Avoid attractive but invalid proxies

A top vote in L18 reflects these participants' preferences, not correctness or authority. A driver/fishbone diagram reflects a theory or hypothesis set, not causation. Supporting-force totals do not establish readiness. A copied positive-deviance practice does not prove improvement. An accepted apology is not restored trust. A completed course or assignment is not demonstrated capability. Ask which later observation would actually bear on the intended outcome, and who bears a balancing cost.

Every catalog profile includes immediate output, later outcome, balancing indicator, baseline/proxy caveat and review timing. Define numeric measures locally in their natural units where useful; do not collect more information than the decision needs.

## A small observation contract

Use this when recommending a trial, not for every conversation or lookup:

1. **Define the opportunity and behavior.** What event counts as a chance to act, and what observable response counts? For a handoff, specify sender readiness, receiver acknowledgement and the agreed deadline. For a help-seeking plan, define the cue before counting attempts.
2. **Locate the comparison.** Use an existing comparable episode when available. Otherwise say baseline unknown and treat the first observations as a starting description, not proof of improvement. Do not infer a baseline from absence of complaints.
3. **Name the proposed observer and window.** For example, the willing task owner reviews their own next three eligible handoffs within two weeks. Name the end event for timing or the denominator for counts. Do not appoint an absent colleague or imply agreement.
4. **Retain exposure separately from response.** Zero eligible opportunities means not tested. Do not report 0% adherence with a zero denominator. At the proposed review date, choose whether to wait for a real opportunity, select a more observable relevant cue, rehearse safely, or stop. Rehearsal is practice, not evidence of workplace transfer.
5. **Check burden and decide.** Define one plausible cost and a locally proposed stop condition. Review whether to retain, adapt or stop with the people affected. One favorable episode can justify another small test, not a causal claim or automatic rollout.

**Compact fictional plan:** “If you both agree, compare acknowledgement by the agreed deadline across the next three eligible handoffs this fortnight; the sender records acknowledgement and the receiver flags extra work. Baseline is unknown. If none occur, mark not tested; if tracking adds more burden than it resolves, stop.” The specific counts and window are illustrative editorial choices, not validated thresholds.
