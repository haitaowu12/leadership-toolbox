# Author examples · fictional

These illustrations were written with the methods. They are not unseen evaluations or reports of workplace effectiveness.

| Situation | A useful first route | Changed fact that changes it |
|---|---|---|
| A capable colleague asks permission on every small decision | L01: clarify the decision boundary and support | If capacity is absent, use L09; if task skill is absent, use L12 |
| Two sponsors want incompatible deadlines | L09 to expose capacity and L04 with the authorised owner | After a decision, L06 communicates the changed plan |
| An experienced person asks to explore options for a meeting | L02, with a person-owned next step | If there is a known required procedure they do not know, teach it with L12 |
| A plan may fail but has not been committed | L13 to surface mechanisms and responses | Active danger takes the established response first |
| A small reversible process change has uncertain effects | L08 with prediction, guardrail and review | If reversal is infeasible or harm cannot be bounded, seek qualified review and the decision owner |
| Work is sent by email and nobody acknowledges | L14 to establish an able recipient and explicit acceptance | If nobody can accept, retain/escalate coverage instead of claiming transfer |
| Current work is progressing, commitments are clear and burden is acceptable | No new intervention; retain the existing checkpoint | A changed commitment may require L06 |

For any example, explain the observed fact, unknowns, next action, alternative, output, later signal and stop/switch reason. Never invent another person’s motives, consent or results.

## Deep interview with a consequential correction (fictional)

This is an author-prepared illustration of desired behavior, not a new model execution or observed human outcome.

1. Agent: “Want the quick route or a deeper challenge? Give me one missed commitment, the outcome you need, and any immediate safety or formal-process concern.”
   User: “Go deep. Alex missed Friday’s draft; I need next Friday’s release. No such concern that I know.”
   Update: deadline reported; support/alignment/authority unknown; voice unassessed. No motivation diagnosis.

2. Agent: “What exactly was agreed, and who can change Alex’s priorities?”
   User: “I set the deadline and own the work.”
   Update: authority reported, provisional; outcome delivery. Candidate set: expectation clarification inside delegation, capacity review, teaching if a skill gap emerges.

3. Agent: “What evidence suggests a skill gap, and when has Alex delivered a similar task?”
   User: “Actually Alex delivers these well. Two other managers added urgent reviews.”
   Update: skill gap unsupported; capacity gap reported; coordination across teams; alignment conflicting priorities. Teaching leaves the shortlist; capacity and decision ownership become leading options.

4. Agent: “You said you own the work. Can you postpone those managers’ requests, and has Alex been able to object?”
   User: “Correction: I only own my project. A director controls all priorities. Alex was criticized last time they pushed back.”
   Update: authority changes to outside user's scope for combined priorities; voice becomes retaliation/coercion concern reported; earlier assertion retained as corrected. Public team-ranking exercise excluded. Prepare a private factual workload brief for the legitimate owner.

5. Agent: “What can the director trade off, and what must not get worse for customers or Alex?”
   User: “Move two reviews, no overtime; release quality must stay the same.”
   Update: explicit constraints; proposal within decision owner's scope; output is revised commitments, not employee reprimand. Pair capacity renegotiation with consultation; a handoff check confirms the resulting owners.

6. Agent: “My reading: the blockage is conflicting assignments, with a reported cost to speaking up. You can propose changes but not impose cross-team priorities. Is anything wrong? I recommend a concise capacity brief for the director, then an accepted commitment record; check workload and quality at the next release.”
   User: “Correct.”
   Plan: prepare the brief; no sending or external action unless authorized. Outcomes: commitments actually changed, next release reviewed; balancing signals: no hidden overtime, shifted overload, or quality decline.


The important change is the action: a reported capacity conflict plus corrected authority and voice concerns changes the shortlist and the format. The agent does not retain an employee-deficit explanation merely because it appeared first. The review checks actual changed commitments, quality and burden, not just whether a meeting occurred.
