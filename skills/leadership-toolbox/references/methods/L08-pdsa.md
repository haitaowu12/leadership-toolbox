# L08 · PDSA bounded test

Version: 0.2.0 · Licence: MIT

**Job:** Test an uncertain process change.

**Use when:** A small reversible test can answer a specific process question.

**Avoid/stop when:** Harm cannot be bounded, necessary authority is absent, or the test bypasses required assurance.

**Prerequisites:** A prediction, a feasible observation and an authorised stop/recovery route.

**Estimated time:** 15–30 minutes planning plus a bounded test window. This is an editorial planning estimate.

**Participants/preparation:** A leader and the relevant person(s); use one concrete task or episode, relevant evidence and the stated prerequisites. Invite access needs and a way to raise concerns privately. Facilitation is required for group input; qualification remains with the task owner.

## Try it

1. Plan a small change and predict an observable result.
2. Do it at limited scale while recording departures from the plan.
3. Study observations against the prediction and alternative explanations.
4. Act: retain, adapt, stop or test again, based on what was learned.

**Words to try:** “What do we predict, what will we observe, and what would make us stop?”

**Immediate output:** A prediction, bounded test record and a next decision.

**One outcome signal:** Did the chosen observation improve relative to the stated baseline during the agreed window? Agree the definition, baseline (or unknown), observation window and review date before acting.

**Guardrail and stop/switch:** Watch shifted workload, quality loss and harm outside the test boundary. Stop when the guardrail is crossed; do not scale from one convenient observation.

## Evidence and origin

Informed by IHI’s Model for Improvement. Published implementation quality varies; using the acronym alone is not a credible experiment.

[Public source](https://www.ihi.org/library/model-for-improvement/testing-changes). This card’s workplace wording, example and observation guidance are editorial applications, not claims by the source provider. No source article or figure is reproduced.

## Fictional example

Try one small change to a recurring meeting for two sessions, recording decision completion and preparation burden before expanding it.

## Counterexample and route change

A one-time test exposes information irreversibly; limiting its duration does not make the harm reversible.
