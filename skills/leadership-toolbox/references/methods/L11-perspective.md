# L11 · Perspective and evidence check

Version: 0.1.0 · Licence: MIT

**Job:** Check a framing before acting.

**Use when:** People appear to disagree because their information, assumptions or constraints may differ.

**Avoid/stop when:** An imagined perspective is being treated as the person’s actual account, or delay would obstruct an urgent established response.

**Prerequisites:** A specific disagreement, distinguishable evidence and access to accounts or explicit acknowledgement they are missing.

**Estimated time:** 10–25 minutes. This is an editorial planning estimate.

**Participants/preparation:** A leader and the relevant person(s); use one concrete task or episode, relevant evidence and the stated prerequisites. Invite access needs and a way to raise concerns privately. Facilitation is required for group input; qualification remains with the task owner.

## Try it

1. Write the observed event separately from your interpretation.
2. Identify which information or assumption would change your view.
3. Ask the other person what they saw, knew and needed.
4. Compare evidence and name unresolved differences.
5. Choose the next inquiry or decision rather than forcing consensus.

**Words to try:** “What information did you have, and what assumption should we check?”

**Immediate output:** A corrected framing, explicit disputed facts or a targeted next inquiry.

**One outcome signal:** Before the decision, were consequential assumptions checked with evidence or the people concerned? Agree the definition, baseline (or unknown), observation window and review date before acting.

**Guardrail and stop/switch:** Watch speculative empathy, endless delay or treating all claims as equally supported. Preserve unknowns and use the appropriate owner when accounts cannot be reconciled.

## Fictional example

Two colleagues disagree about a delay. Compare what each knew when they made the plan before assigning fault.
