# L21 · Current-state process map

Version: 0.2.0 · Licence: MIT

**Job:** Understand actual steps, waits and rework loops.

**Prerequisites:** A bounded start/end, access to appropriate non-sensitive examples and participation from people who do the work.

**Estimated time:** 30–60 minutes for initial preparation/session, not total implementation time. Editorial planning range; adapt to accessibility and complexity.

**Participants/preparation:** Include the legitimate owner and the people whose knowledge or acceptance the card requires. Share the purpose and access options; no disclosure or agreement is assumed.

**Use / avoid.** Use when delays, rework, or handoff friction span several roles. Avoid mapping the official procedure from memory and presenting it as observed reality, or documenting sensitive individual cases unnecessarily.

**Run.** Choose an explicit start and end. Gather people who perform different parts of the work. Sketch the major steps before adding detail. Walk a few real examples through the sequence, including exceptions, decisions, waiting, returns, and duplicate entry. Mark who acts and what information moves at each transition. Separate work time from waiting time where data permit. Confirm disagreements through observation rather than majority vote. Identify one bottleneck or rework loop to investigate; design a future-state change only after agreeing the current state.

**Output / distinction.** A validated current-state process map with one focused improvement opportunity. Unlike a handoff protocol, this examines the end-to-end system and its loops rather than specifying one transfer.

**Primary source.** Institute for Healthcare Improvement, [Flowchart](https://www.ihi.org/library/tools/flowchart). **Claim / limit:** distinguishes high-level and detailed maps and describes identifying complexity and non-value-adding steps. A map is a representation, not time-series evidence; observing a few cases can miss rare but consequential pathways.

**Words to try:** “Where does work wait or come back, and how do you know that is the actual route?”

**One outcome signal:** At the next review, check a selected waiting/rework loop against actual cases with start/end definitions and denominators. Agree a baseline (or unknown), observer, observation window and review date before action.

**Balancing guardrail and stop/switch:** No unnecessary personal case data, pressure to follow the idealised map, or optimising one queue by shifting work elsewhere. If a prerequisite fails, prepare the missing evidence or use the appropriate owner/process before committing.

## Example and counterexample (fictional)

An onboarding map discovers that a request waits for a second approval that nobody thought existed.

Counterexample: A leader copies the official procedure and calls it verified without asking the people performing it.
