# L22 · Force-field change diagnosis

Version: 0.2.0 · Licence: MIT

**Job:** Identify present enablers and constraints to change.

**Prerequisites:** A specific proposed change and affected perspectives; no assumption that the proposal must proceed.

**Estimated time:** 20–45 minutes for initial preparation/session, not total implementation time. Editorial planning range; adapt to accessibility and complexity.

**Participants/preparation:** Include the legitimate owner and the people whose knowledge or acceptance the card requires. Share the purpose and access options; no disclosure or agreement is assumed.

**Use / avoid.** Use when a proposed change is clear but progress is stalled by interacting enablers and constraints. Avoid using the exercise to score people as enemies or to claim numerical certainty about political or cultural factors.

**Run.** State the specific proposed change and the present situation. Independently list forces that support it and forces that constrain it. Combine duplicates while preserving disagreements. Check whose perspective is missing. Rate the relative significance of each force using one consistent scale, recording the rationale and uncertainty. Distinguish forces the team can influence from conditions it must accommodate. Choose one important barrier to reduce and one supporting condition to protect. Assign actions and revisit whether those forces actually changed.

**Output / distinction.** A change-readiness diagnosis and targeted enabling actions. Unlike a premortem, it examines forces operating now; unlike interest negotiation, it covers systemic constraints as well as stakeholder concerns.

**Primary source.** Central and North West London NHS Foundation Trust, [Force Field Analysis](https://www.cnwl.nhs.uk/ia/resources/force-field-analysis). **Claim / limit:** documents supportive and opposing forces, relative weighting, and removing barriers. Ratings structure discussion; they are not measurements whose totals validly establish a probability of success or an automatic go/no-go decision.

**Adapt the execution:** Replace labels such as staff resistance with a specific condition and whose account supports it. Identify an authorized response and what would show that work is now possible. Do not total forces into readiness probabilities.

**Words to try:** “Which constraint can we actually reduce, rather than simply push harder against?”

**One outcome signal:** At review, check whether the selected barrier/enabler actually changed and whether the change became more feasible. Agree a baseline (or unknown), observer, observation window and review date before action.

**Balancing guardrail and stop/switch:** No sums predicting success, labeling people as enemies, or pressure to suppress legitimate objections. If a prerequisite fails, prepare the missing evidence or use the appropriate owner/process before committing.

## Example and counterexample (fictional)

A shared tool stalls because training time and access approvals are missing; owners address those conditions.

Counterexample: The sponsor treats high supporting-force scores as permission to override serious objections.
