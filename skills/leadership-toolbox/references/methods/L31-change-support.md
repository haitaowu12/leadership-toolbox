# L31 · Change impact and support planning

Version: 0.2.1 · Licence: MIT

**Distinct job:** turn a defined change into a realistic transition for affected people. Output: an impact/support plan, honest communication commitments, and feedback/adjustment route. Force-field analysis diagnoses present enablers/barriers; this specifies concrete consequences and support.

**Use/prerequisites:** a sufficiently defined proposal, named owner, known decision boundaries, affected groups who can contribute safely, and resources to address concerns. Do not pretend a fixed decision is negotiable or substitute communication for changing an unworkable proposal.

## Try it

1. Separate what is decided, proposed, and unknown, and explain why change is considered.
2. For each affected group, map changed tasks, workload, skills, interfaces, access, and losses.
3. Ask, “What will become harder, and what support would actually help?”
4. Agree training, capacity, tools, transitions, and responsible owners.
5. Communicate impacts, timeline, uncertainties, and when updates will arrive.
6. Check actual effects and respond to concerns after implementation.

**Editorial burden:** 30–60 minutes preparation, focused 20–40 minute conversations by group, scheduled post-change review.

**Checks:** immediate: material impacts have owned responses. Later: work can be performed under changed conditions. Balance: hidden overtime, excluded voices, unresolved losses, or performative consultation.

**Matching:** cross-boundary coordination, mixed knowledge, support gaps, or conflicting priorities; use safe listening first where needed.

**Counterexample:** sending more persuasive messages cannot fix inaccessible tooling.

**Adapt the execution:** Check an ordinary task for each affected role under actual new conditions: access, understanding, affordable time and a usable exception route. If performance is infeasible, revise support or the proposal rather than intensify messaging.

**Words to try:** “What will become harder, and what support would actually help?”

## Fictional example

A new rota includes training, access changes, workload cover and a date to review actual effects.

## Stop, review and combine

Stop or switch when the stated prerequisites fail, the user lacks authority for the proposed commitment, privacy/voice conditions make the interaction unsafe, or the method delays a required protective/specialist process. Set a named review owner and a locally appropriate date or event; retain unknown baselines rather than invent them. The time range in the catalog combines the bounded initial preparation and conversation, excludes downstream implementation, and is an editorial estimate.

Related alternatives: [Force-field change diagnosis (L22)](L22-force-field.md), [Stakeholder map and listening round (L17)](L17-stakeholders.md). Use a sequence only when one method produces a needed prerequisite; name the owner and transition condition.

**One outcome signal:** Observe supported task completion and workload/error/access guardrails separately from approval of the change. Agree the definition, baseline (or unknown), observer, observation window and review trigger before action.
