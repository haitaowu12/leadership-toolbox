# L04 · Interest-based negotiation

Version: 0.2.0 · Licence: MIT

**Job:** Explore incompatible legitimate interests and decide whether an agreement is worth making.

**Use when:** Parties have different requests and can genuinely consider options. Distinguish a resources conflict from a disagreement about facts.

**Avoid/stop when:** Safety or rights are being bargained away, participation is coerced, the user cannot authorise a commitment, or alleged misconduct needs an independent formal route.

**Prerequisites:** Named decision owners, negotiable boundaries, relevant affected-party input and a realistic alternative if no agreement is reached.

**Estimated time:** 20–45 minutes for a bounded issue, plus preparation. Editorial planning estimate; complex disputes require more support.

**Participants/preparation:** Relevant parties or legitimate representatives. Separately record interests, constraints, evidence, decision rights and each party's realistic no-agreement alternative. Provide accessible/private ways to raise concerns. Do not assume equal bargaining power.

## Try it

1. Describe the shared issue without character judgments. Ask why each requested position matters.
2. Distinguish genuine constraints from preferences; check missing stakeholders and downstream costs.
3. Before negotiating, identify your realistic no-agreement alternative, its costs and the conditions under which declining would be better. Do not bluff about alternatives.
4. Generate several feasible options before committing. Compare them with agreed defensible criteria and the alternative, including implementation burden.
5. Check authority and voluntary acceptance. Record either a bounded agreement, a request to the authorised owner, or a reasoned decision not to agree.
6. For an agreement, specify owner, resources, checkpoint and conditions for reopening. A promise unsupported by capacity remains unresolved.

**Adapt the execution:** Separate facts to verify, interests to negotiate, authority to clarify and protections that cannot be traded. Generate possibilities before evaluating them. Compare packages when priorities differ; disclose a genuinely fixed tradeoff. Ask about process preferences rather than inferring them from culture.

**Words to try:** “What must this option protect for you? If we cannot agree, what realistically happens, and is this proposal actually better?”

**Immediate output:** An explicit disposition with rationale and responsibilities. A reasoned no-agreement outcome can be useful.

**One outcome signal:** At the agreed checkpoint, have the accepted commitments protected the named interests within their constraints? If no agreement, review whether the stated alternative remains feasible. Baseline, observer, period and review date are locally agreed.

**Guardrail and stop/switch:** Watch hidden losers, pressured assent and unconsulted dependencies. Stop if an option exceeds authority or trades away protections. Use L09 for an unresolved workload tradeoff, L11 for missing accounts, or a qualified independent route when negotiation is inappropriate.

## Evidence and origin

Original explanation of the principled-negotiation tradition associated with Fisher, Ury and Patton. [PON's principles](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/tag/principled-negotiation/) describe interests, options and criteria; [PON's agreement-trap discussion](https://www.pon.harvard.edu/daily/batna/too-eager-to-close-nb/) explains why agreement is not itself success. Practitioner guidance does not prove this script's effectiveness or guarantee compatible interests.

No source article, diagram, exercise or simulation is reproduced. Workplace prompts, timing, metrics and examples are this project's editorial application. Linked material retains its own rights.

## Fictional example and counterexample

Two departments need the same specialist on Friday. One requires an external deadline; the other's date is convenient. They consider a split, reduced scope and a later slot, then compare these against the resource owner's fallback. If every option creates unsafe hours, they decline the proposed deal and escalate the unresolved priority.

A complaint involving retaliation is a counterexample: do not arrange a joint “win-win” conversation in place of an independent reporting and protection process.
