# L05 · 1-2-4-All

Version: 0.1.0 · Licence: CC-BY-SA-4.0

**Job:** Hear ideas beyond dominant voices.

**Use when:** A group needs contributions and the question benefits from several perspectives.

**Avoid/stop when:** Confidential matters cannot be shared, participation is coerced, or the decision has already been made without disclosure.

**Prerequisites:** A clear invitation, accessible participation and an explicit explanation of how input will be used.

**Estimated time:** About 15–20 minutes; adapt for access needs. This is an editorial planning estimate.

**Participants/preparation:** A leader and the relevant person(s); use one concrete task or episode, relevant evidence and the stated prerequisites. Invite access needs and a way to raise concerns privately. Facilitation is required for group input; qualification remains with the task owner.

## Try it

1. Invite quiet individual thinking on one concrete question.
2. Let pairs exchange ideas, then groups of four combine what they learned.
3. Bring selected ideas to the whole group.
4. Record objections and explain which decision owner will use the input.

**Words to try:** “What could we change so this next step works better for everyone affected?”

**Immediate output:** A set of ideas and unresolved concerns with a visible disposition route.

**One outcome signal:** Can people who usually speak less point to an idea or concern that was heard and considered? Agree the definition, baseline (or unknown), observation window and review date before acting.

**Guardrail and stop/switch:** Watch forced disclosure, inaccessible timing and consultation with no influence. Offer a private/accessibility alternative and be honest about decision limits.

## Evidence and origin

Procedural adaptation of the public Liberating Structures method. Participation counts alone do not demonstrate decision quality or safety.

[Public source](https://www.liberatingstructures.com/1-2-4-all). This card’s workplace wording, example and observation guidance are editorial applications, not claims by the source provider. No source article or figure is reproduced.

## Fictional example

A team is choosing improvements to a routine meeting. Use a concrete question, then explain which ideas will be tried and why.
