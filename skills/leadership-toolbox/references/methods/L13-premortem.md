# L13 · Premortem

Version: 0.2.0 · Licence: MIT

**Job:** Surface failure risks before commitment.

**Use when:** A proposed plan is concrete enough to examine and concerns may be staying unspoken.

**Avoid/stop when:** It becomes a blame exercise, an exhaustive risk assessment is legally required or no owner can respond to findings.

**Prerequisites:** A candidate plan, participants with relevant knowledge and an owner for responses.

**Estimated time:** 20–40 minutes. This is an editorial planning estimate.

**Participants/preparation:** A leader and the relevant person(s); use one concrete task or episode, relevant evidence and the stated prerequisites. Invite access needs and a way to raise concerns privately. Facilitation is required for group input; qualification remains with the task owner.

## Try it

1. Imagine the plan has failed at a defined future point.
2. Let people independently suggest plausible reasons before discussion.
3. Compare the mechanisms and supporting evidence.
4. Choose the consequential preventable risks and assign a mitigation, early warning or further check.
5. Update the plan and retain unresolved risks.

**Words to try:** “Imagine this plan has failed by the review date: what mechanism could have caused it?”

**Immediate output:** A short set of failure mechanisms with owners and responses.

**One outcome signal:** Before commitment, did the plan change to address a consequential plausible failure, and is its warning monitored? Agree the definition, baseline (or unknown), observation window and review date before acting.

**Guardrail and stop/switch:** Watch pessimism without evidence, false reassurance or suppression of dissent. This supplements required risk processes and cannot certify safety.

Original summary informed by Gary Klein’s public premortem description. It is a risk-discovery aid, not a validated probability estimator.

## Fictional example

Before changing a recurring service, invite possible failure mechanisms and decide which need a small test or safeguard.

## Counterexample and route change

A premortem produces a risk list but nobody can change the plan; obtain an authorised disposition rather than claim assurance.
