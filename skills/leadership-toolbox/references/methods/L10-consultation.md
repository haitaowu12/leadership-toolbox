# L10 · Evidence and consultation

Version: 0.1.0 · Licence: MIT

**Job:** Obtain cooperation across boundaries.

**Use when:** You need a decision or cooperation from people you do not direct.

**Avoid/stop when:** Consent is being manipulated, the request bypasses a required owner or evidence is being selectively presented.

**Prerequisites:** A clear ask, relevant evidence and access to affected voices or an honest account of missing consultation.

**Estimated time:** 20–40 minutes preparation plus conversations. This is an editorial planning estimate.

**Participants/preparation:** A leader and the relevant person(s); use one concrete task or episode, relevant evidence and the stated prerequisites. Invite access needs and a way to raise concerns privately. Facilitation is required for group input; qualification remains with the task owner.

## Try it

1. Identify the actual decision owner and who bears the consequences.
2. Ask affected people about needs and concerns instead of inventing their views.
3. Present options, evidence, uncertainty and costs.
4. Make a concrete authorised ask.
5. Record the choice, rationale, owner and review trigger in a lightweight decision note.

**Words to try:** “What evidence or concern would change this decision?”

**Immediate output:** A decision request or decision record with evidence, concerns and follow-up.

**One outcome signal:** By the review date, is there an explicit decision/response and do affected people understand its consequences? Agree the definition, baseline (or unknown), observation window and review date before acting.

**Guardrail and stop/switch:** Watch pressure, omitted objections and commitments outside your remit. A refusal or no decision is a real disposition, not a fabricated success.

## Fictional example

A partner function controls a needed resource. Ask what would make the request workable, then present options and uncertainty to the owner.
