# L29 · Options and criteria trade-off review

Version: 0.2.0 · Licence: MIT

**Distinct job:** compare feasible alternatives before a significant decision. Output: a decision brief with constraints, options, evidence, uncertainty, consequences, owner, and rationale. Different from nominal-group prioritization (participant preferences) and consultation (who contributes/decides).

**Use/prerequisites:** a bounded decision, legitimate owner, materially different alternatives, evidence or explicit uncertainty, and enough time for analysis. Avoid elaborate analysis for trivial choices; unresolved criteria call for framing rather than invented weights.

**Run:** (1) State the decision, latest useful time, owner, and must-not-violate constraints. (2) Include credible alternatives, including defer/do-minimum where feasible. (3) Agree decision-specific criteria in their natural units and screen hard constraints separately. (4) Compare evidence, ranges, downstream effects, and missing information. (5) Ask, “Which uncertain assumption could reverse this choice?” Test sensitivity; preserve non-comparable trade-offs. (6) Let the owner decide and record reasons, dissent, and review triggers.

**Editorial burden:** 20–60 minutes preparation, 30–60 minutes review for a bounded decision; substantial technical choices need specialist work.

**Checks:** immediate: alternatives and decisive trade-offs explicit. Later: review tests original assumptions. Balance: delay, false precision, omitted stakeholders/harms.

**Matching:** useful with expert-analysis/mixed knowledge and significant consequences; no automatic numeric threshold. Requires authority; cannot replace required expert review.

**Counterexample:** a weighted score cannot compensate for violating a safety requirement.

**Source/limit:** NASA, [6.8 Decision Analysis](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-8-decision-analysis/), documents criteria, alternatives, uncertainty and sensitivity. It does not validate arbitrary weights or global leadership-method ranking.

**Adapt the execution:** Write what plausible new evidence or value tradeoff would make another option preferable. Keep the dated rationale so outcome luck is not mistaken for decision-process quality; equivalent framing is a consistency check.

**Words to try:** “Which uncertain assumption could reverse this choice?”

## Fictional example

A team compares maintaining, replacing or repairing a tool against cost, reliability and disruption; the owner records the tradeoff.

## Stop, review and combine

Stop or switch when the stated prerequisites fail, the user lacks authority for the proposed commitment, privacy/voice conditions make the interaction unsafe, or the method delays a required protective/specialist process. Set a named review owner and a locally appropriate date or event; retain unknown baselines rather than invent them. The time range in the catalog combines the bounded initial preparation and conversation, excludes downstream implementation, and is an editorial estimate.

Related alternatives: [L18](L18-nominal-group.md), [L13](L13-premortem.md). Use a sequence only when one method produces a needed prerequisite; name the owner and transition condition.
