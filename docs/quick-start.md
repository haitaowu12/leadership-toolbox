# Your first useful session

Use the [installation guide](installation.md) for a complete skill folder or cloud upload. Start a new conversation after enabling it. Do not upload confidential workplace material just to try it; use a fictional or de-identified situation.

## 1. Ask for a concrete next move

Prompt:

> Use leadership-toolbox. Two delivery dates slipped. I am preparing a check-in, but I have not heard the team's account yet. Give me a short opening and a sensible next step.

A useful answer should separate the missed dates from an explanation such as low ownership. It can propose asking for a walkthrough of the latest episode before choosing between workload, decision-rights, coordination or skill remedies. It should not invent a blockage or promise to remove one.

## 2. Get probing without asking for it

Prompt:

> We keep missing handoffs and I think people do not take ownership. What should I do?

Expect one to three consequential questions, preferably one, before a full recommendation. Answer with a concrete episode, then correct an important fact and check that the advice changes. No special “grill me” wording is needed. “Keep it short” should shorten the question, not eliminate it. You can still ask for deeper challenge. You can say “skip that,” “stop questioning,” or “give me a provisional answer.” The skill should honor that choice.

## 3. Request a direct explanation

Prompt:

> Explain GROW with an example and a source. What does the research not establish?

This prompt explicitly asks about research: expect a direct explanation and an underlying primary source, with a distinction between the named procedure, broader coaching research and evidence from multicomponent programs. A method lookup should not force an intake form or a practice log.

For an ordinary lookup such as “Explain a closed-loop handoff”, expect a plain-language explanation and normally just the practical method-wiki link. Healthcare and safety references should not be its front door.

## 4. Make any trial observable

Prompt:

> Help me make that next step testable without adding a reporting burden. We have no baseline yet.

A useful plan defines the next actual opportunity, what would be observed, a proposed observer, a comparison or unknown baseline, a review point and a burden guardrail. No opportunity means **not tested**. A completed conversation does not establish later improvement.

## 5. Make the situation visible and correct it

Prompt:

> Show your current understanding as a small context diagram before advising. Mark what I reported, what you assumed and what is unknown. Ask me to correct the most important relationship.

Then correct an actor, edge or boundary:

> Correction: finance owns approval, not me. I coordinate the project and cannot delegate its decision. Update your map, then show the problem hypotheses and conditional solution space. No more questions for now.

Expect stable IDs for unchanged actors, removal of the stale authority edge, a short change record, and revised narrative/method eligibility. Current facts, hypotheses and proposed options should stay distinct. A delegation recommendation must change when the user lacks that authority. Read the [worked example](../skills/leadership-toolbox/references/visual-examples.md) and [optional model template](../skills/leadership-toolbox/templates/situation-model.md).

The complete text node/relationship list works without a diagram renderer. Mermaid is optional; this skill does not install a renderer, code interpreter or tenant capability. Ask for one or two useful views rather than a wall of diagrams. A simple request can still receive no diagram.

## Check that it loaded

Ask:

> Which version of leadership-toolbox did you load? Open its L14 handoff card and describe one prerequisite and one limit.

Confirm version 0.2.1 and an actual reference read. A confident answer alone is insufficient; use the host's file/tool activity when available. If the assistant cannot access linked files, provide the complete folder through the host's supported route rather than pretending the entrypoint alone contains the library.

## Keep it proportionate

A good outcome may be one useful question, a clearer decision, supported practice, a small coordination change, or no added process. Serious safety concerns and allegations need the appropriate protected/qualified process, not a generic group exercise. These examples describe intended behavior; [validation](validation.md) distinguishes executed checks from desired behavior and human outcomes.
